The insight: you need two different share artifacts, for two different people

Your problem all along has been that head-to-head produces losers, and losers don't amplify. Word clouds are non-zero-sum — every company gets its own, nobody gets K.O.'d.

Artifact 1 — for the user. After ~15 swipes, hand them a result about themselves: "You're a contrarian. You backed the underdog in 9 of 15." Or a taste profile: "Your picks skew infra over consumer." People share things that say something about them, not things that say something about a company. This is the mechanic every viral quiz has ever used.

Artifact 2 — for the company. "What 8,400 people say about Linear, in one word." That's an image a founder screenshots and posts themselves, unprompted, for free. You've just recruited the exact distributors you couldn't reach before — and they arrive without you having to email 64 people.

One loop, two amplification channels, both free. That's the whole strategy.

The loop I'd actually build

Land → swipe head-to-heads immediately, no account, no paywall → after ~10 swipes, a soft prompt: "one word for Zepto" → after ~15, your personal result card + share → and every company page carries its live word cloud, permanently, as a shareable object.

Both the swipe and the word are free and instant. The word is the higher-value data and it comes after you've earned engagement.

What this turns your HN post into

Right now you'd be posting "I built a site where you pay a dollar to vote," which gets eaten alive. Instead you'd be posting:

Show HN: 2M pairwise comparisons of YC companies — here's the ranking and the raw data

Methodology writeup, Elo explanation, the surprising results, CSV download. That's a front-page post. HN doesn't upvote games; it upvotes datasets with a defensible method and an author who publishes the ugly parts. You'd also have a genuinely interesting thing to say — pairwise-comparison ranking is a real technique with real failure modes, and explaining how you handled ballot-stuffing and transitivity violations is the post.

Where the money comes from instead

The word cloud is quietly a brand perception tracker, and that's a real business. Companies already pay agencies five figures a year to learn what four adjectives the market associates with them. You'd have it live, longitudinal, and free to collect.

So: consumer side always free; sell the dashboard to the companies. Sentiment over time, word drift after a launch, benchmarking against a competitor set. Also sponsored or custom brackets — a VC firm paying to run its portfolio through the machine.

That's a far better business than $0.99 votes, and it's the identical product. You just stop charging the audience and start charging the subjects.

Two things that will bite you

Moderation. Free-text words about real companies will produce "scam," "fraud," slurs, and coordinated brigading. Elegant fix: a word only enters the cloud at a threshold — say 5+ independent submissions. That auto-filters garbage, makes every visible word actually meaningful, and gives you a clean answer when a company complains. Add a profanity filter and rate limiting on top. Label it "community perception, unverified."

Ballot stuffing. The moment a company notices its cloud, someone will script it. You need per-session limits, basic fingerprinting, and — importantly — publish that you do. HN will ask; having an answer ready is worth more than the answer being perfect.

Build order
Swipe, free, no account. Elo ranking behind it.
Personal result card + share (this is the user-side loop).
One-word prompt with threshold moderation.
Company word cloud pages with dynamic OG images.
Then the HN post, framed as data.
Company dashboard, once you have volume worth selling.

The modal in your screenshot doesn't survive any of this — and that's the point. The disabled CONTINUE sitting behind a disclaimer checkbox is a friction gate protecting a dollar you shouldn't be collecting. Take the money out of the loop and the loop can finally spin.
